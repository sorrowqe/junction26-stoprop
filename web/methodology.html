Methodology

The full write-up (pipeline, caching, privacy, evaluation protocol and known limitations) is being written. These principles are fixed:

  1. How, not what. We label techniques and behaviours, never people, outlets or viewpoints.
  2. The AI proposes, code verifies. Every highlighted passage must appear word for word in your text; positions are computed by our code.
  3. Reported speech is not the author's manipulation. Words attributed to someone else (direct quotes and attributed indirect speech such as “X said that…” or “according to X…”) are marked and not counted. Vague attributions like “experts say” do not count as reported speech.
  4. Abstain instead of guessing. “Nothing found”, “insufficient data” and “not checked” are valid answers.
  5. Two independent signals before calling anything “likely coordinated”.
  6. Only show what we can measure. Techniques below 70% measured precision are experimental and hidden by default.
  7. No single “propaganda score”. Profiles and evidence instead.
  8. Colours describe, they don't judge. Amber means “no source given”, not “false”; green means “source given”, not “true”.
  9. Innocent explanations are listed next to every flag.
  10. Privacy by default. No accounts; swarm uploads are processed in memory.

Language coverage: any language is accepted, but accuracy has only been measured for English. Other languages are marked as not measured (see Metrics).

Sensitivity levels are calibrated on the English development set: strict (the default) targets at most 5% of clean news paragraphs flagged with a technique, balanced at most 15%, and broad shows every label the AI proposes. The measured trade-off for each level, with confidence intervals, is on the Metrics page.

Swarm mode looks at a batch of posts, never a single one. Four independent signal families: text reuse (character 5-gram MinHash, ≥ 60% overlap, posts of at least 8 words, different accounts only), timing (60% of a group's posts in a window far tighter than the batch's normal pace, Poisson p < 0.001, at least 3 accounts), accounts (shared name pattern, creation dates within 7 days, single-purpose accounts) and amplification (the same non-mainstream link, hashtag or mention pushed by at least 5 accounts). “Likely coordinated” needs text reuse plus one other family; text reuse alone is “worth watching”. Shared news headlines, generic phrases, quoted statements and event hashtags lower the verdict and are shown as innocent explanations. Verdicts describe groups and behaviour, never individual people.

Where swarm posts come from. Paste a link and Stoprop reads the post and its replies through public interfaces: Bluesky's public AppView API (no login), the Mastodon server's own public API (many servers share at most 60 replies with visitors who are not logged in; we say so), and the YouTube Data API (comments, with a server key). Each run is limited to 2000 posts, 40 API requests and 25 seconds; collected posts stay in memory for 10 minutes and are never written to disk. X (Twitter), Facebook, Instagram, Threads, TikTok and Reddit do not allow outside tools to read posts without login or paid/approved API access, so they are not supported; export the posts yourself and upload a file instead. We do not scrape logged-in pages. Groups of the same accounts posting several messages are shown as one campaign.

No AI-generated-text detection: such detectors are unreliable and known to be biased against non-native writers.

Stoprop labels techniques and behaviours, never people, outlets or viewpoints.
