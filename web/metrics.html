Metrics

How well Stoprop works, measured on labelled data: precision and recall per technique, false positives on neutral text, a symmetry test, and cost. We publish the weak spots too.

Read this first

  • English only. Stoprop accepts any language, but accuracy has only been measured for English. Estonian, Russian and other languages are not measured.
  • Model overlap: the analyzer now runs on gemini-3.5-flash-lite, and a Gemini model (gemini-3.5-flash) was one of the two reviewers that validated the synthetic test items. A model family can favour its own style, so the synthetic scores may be flattered. The SemEval scores are not affected: those labels come from human annotators.
  • Synthetic test items were written by a model (gpt-5.5) from fixed specifications and validated by two independent model families (Gemini and Mistral), consensus only, not human-verified. Items the two reviewers disagreed on are excluded.
  • SemEval strict vs. lenient: “strict” does not count techniques inside quoted or attributed speech (our rule: reported speech is not the author's manipulation), while “lenient” counts them, as the SemEval-2020 annotators did.
  • False positives are measured on SemEval paragraphs that human annotators labelled as containing no technique, and separately on synthetic hard negatives (emotional but factual reports, quoted politicians, fair opinion pieces).
  • Synthetic data measures the mechanics, not how often manipulation occurs in the real world.
  • SemEval is a hard benchmark. In the SemEval-2020 Task 11 competition the best system for finding propaganda spans (ignoring which technique) reached F1 51.7%, and the best system for naming the technique of spans it was given reached micro-F1 63.7% (corrected results, Da San Martino et al., 2020, arXiv:2009.02696). Stoprop must find the span and name the technique at once, on a sample mapped to our labels and scored with a different overlap rule, so the numbers are context, not a like-for-like comparison.
